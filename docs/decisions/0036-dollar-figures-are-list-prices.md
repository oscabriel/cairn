# Dollar figures are list prices, labelled, and unknown is not zero

pi-ai's cost is a list price, not a bill, and every provider the user has is a plan: the bridge records zero, OpenCode Go and Codex are subscriptions. So every dollar figure on screen is labelled "at API list price" unless a provider is metered, and Claude's tokens are shown as that labelled equivalent so one dollar budget spans every role. A model without a price counts tokens only and is shown as unpriced, never as zero.

Spend is a derived cache recomputed from each conversation's `pi.usage` document, so a `kill -9` cannot double count. When a budget is reached, a workflow aborts its running nodes, keeps their partial results and offers a raise as an approval; a goal thread's own subagents stop taking new work and let running children finish. Overshoot is at most one response per running child, and the card says so.

Decided by the user on 2026-10-27, from the phase 2 area notes.

Gate evidence (package 4E, 2026-10-29). J-spend: `judges/spend-kill.sh` passes (12 kills over 48 turns, 96 responses, 0 failures; control failed by double counts only); its last life now retries what the kills parked, and with `--quick` (kills 50 to 150 ms after producing) 4 parked tasks were retried and the case passed, while the same run without the retry never finished. J-budget: `node judges/budget-kill.ts` passes in each of five runs of its kills case after the review fixes: ten faux children under a `stop` budget of four children's worth (102,000 micros at list price, 3,950 micros a response), killed twice at random and once inside the guard's stop pass (a run whose kills miss that window runs again, and fails after three), overshot by 16,500 to 24,400 micros against an allowance of 39,500, one response per running child; no child answered after the restart, the stopped notice appeared once, at most one push went out, the row read exhausted, and after the raise was answered as the user the row read within and a child sent more work answered. Its controls fail for their reasons: a guard that stops nothing overshot by 169,550 to 216,950 micros, and a last life booted without the budget repair (killed at the start of the stop pass) let children answer after the restart. The guard coalesces the ledger's changes, so it keeps up with a burst of responses instead of acting on each in turn.

Readings for sign-off at integration (package 4E). A subagent's own brief budget is a `stop` scope: at its line the child's run is stopped and its tools refused, which is what bounds J-budget's overshoot; the sentence above about letting running children finish is read as the goal's budget over its subagents, a `drain` scope, where running children finish under their own budgets. In a `drain` scope the head thread keeps its own non-delegating tools past the line, so its own spend is not bounded by the gate; only a `stop` scope bounds every conversation in it. An accepted raise that leaves a scope at its line is a new epoch at the line: the guard writes a new notice and asks a new raise, each proposing the larger of double the line and what was spent plus one more line.

Accepted at the wave 4 integration (2026-10-29), with the readings above. On `main`, with the budget gate now in the production extension set: `node judges/budget-kill.ts` kills pass (3 kills, one inside the stop pass, 32,300 micros over against 39,500 allowed; raise admitted), no-stop and no-boot fail by overshoot and restart; `judges/spend-kill.sh` kills pass (12 kills, 48 turns, 80 responses, 4 parked tasks retried), the control fails by double counts only. Faux model, $0.
